A connective binds together a number of propositonal terms into a single, compound proposition called an expression. 

"not x" is denoted as $¬x$, and often termed logical complement (or negation) 

"x and y" is denoted as $x \wedge y$ and is termed as logical conjunction

"x or y" is denoted as $x \lor y$ and is termed as logical **inclusive disjunction** 

"x or y but not x and y" is denoted as $x \oplus y$ and is often called excluse-or and termed logical **exclusive disjunction** 

"x implies y" is denoted as $x\implies y$ and sometimes written as "if $x$ then $y$" and termed **logical implication**

"x is equivalent to y" is denoted as $x \equiv y$ and is sometimes written as "x if and only if y" or even "x iff. y" termed logical equivalence. 

# Precedence
Statements are grouped using parentheses when there could be confusion about the ordering which logical expressions are to be evaluated.  For example, writing:
$$
(x \wedge y) \lor z
$$
Meaning the conjunction of $x$ and y is resolved first. However no parenthesis are not included, we rely on precedence rules to determine the order for us. In short:

Negation (complement), Conjunction, Disjunction, Implication and equivalence

1. $¬$
2. $\wedge$
3. $\lor$
4. $\implies$
5. $\equiv$
