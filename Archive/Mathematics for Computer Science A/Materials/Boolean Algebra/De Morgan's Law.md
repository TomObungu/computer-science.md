De Morgan's laws are another set of laws explaining how disjunction and conjunction relate to one another. Much like validity and satisfiability, conjunction and disjunction form a duality - they are mirror images of one another. Conjunction is false when either conjunct is false and disjunction is false when both disjuncts are false. De Morgan's laws express this duality using negation:
$$
\begin{gather*}
¬(\phi \land \psi) \equiv ¬ \phi \lor ¬\psi \\ \\
¬(\phi \lor \psi) \equiv ¬ \phi \land ¬\psi
\end{gather*}
$$
