Often called RHEL, is a commercial Linux distribution built by Red Hat. 

![[Pasted image 20260714110255.png]]
It is designed for organisations that need long support windows, predictable releases, security maintenance and professional support. 

It is used across servers, data centres, cloud systems and regulated business environments. 

## Why RHEL is important

RHEL is important as it gives organisations a stable and supported platform for production workloads. 

The focus is not simply on having Linux but on having Linux with enterprise expectations around reliability and support. 

# Red Hat and Fedora

RHEL is connected to broader Red Hat ecosystem. Fedora is part of Red Hat and is the free, community driven development ground for Red Hat. 

New Linux technologies, kernels and packages are are introduced in Fedora, then rigorously tested and refined before eventually being incorporated into the commercial, enterprise-grade RHEL. [[Fedora]]

# Package Management 
RHEL uses the RPM package format and tools such as DNF to install, update and manage software. This places it in the same general package familiy as Fedora and openSUSE. 

# Certifications

RHEL is also closely associated with professional training and certification. Credentials such as RHCSA and RHCE are well known in Linux administration and are part of why RHEL remains highly visible in professional environments.
